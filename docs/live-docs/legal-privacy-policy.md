# Lumo Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-03 · Draft for legal review (LUM-857)

This policy explains what personal data [Lumo legal entity], [registered address] ("Lumo", "we", "us") collects when you use the Lumo website, web application, command-line tool (CLI) and integrations (together, the "Service"), why we collect it, how long we keep it, who we share it with, and the rights you have.

## Who is responsible for your data

- **Account data** (your name, email address and sign-in details): Lumo is the controller.
- **Workspace data** (everything your team puts into or connects to a Lumo workspace, including code): the customer that owns the workspace is the controller and Lumo is its processor. We process it only to provide the Service and on the customer's instructions, under our Data Processing Agreement (DPA). If you are a member of someone else's workspace, contact that workspace's owner first about your data in it; we will help them respond.

### AI processing

When an AI feature runs, the relevant text (which may include code) is sent to a large language model through the Vercel AI Gateway. Except for boundary-crossing triage (below), every request is sent with zero data retention enforced: it is only routed to model hosts that have agreed not to store the data or train on it, and it fails rather than going to a host that would. Please note:

- Boundary-crossing triage is the one exception. When Lumo's rule detectors flag an action an AI agent took, Lumo asks TypeSafe's Jev model, through the Vercel AI Gateway, whether the action was a legitimate part of the task. For this it sends the flagged command or file path (with detected credentials redacted), the project rule that fired, the task's title and description, and the agent's own explanation. Jev's only host, DigitalOcean, has committed not to train on the data but does not offer zero data retention. Turning off AI review in workspace settings stops this triage.
- OpenAI's zero-data-retention terms allow limited retention for abuse monitoring ("safety retention").
- Open-weight models (such as the DeepSeek and GLM families) are run by third-party hosting providers listed on our subprocessor page, not by the model authors.
- AI requests are routed globally, so they may be processed outside the region where you are located. Zero data retention applies wherever a request is processed, but we cannot currently commit to a location for AI processing.

## How long we keep data

| Data | Retention |
| --- | --- |
| Account data | While your account is active. We delete it on request, unless we must keep some of it to meet a legal obligation. |
| Workspace content | For as long as the workspace exists. Older document revisions are thinned automatically (one per day for the last week, then one per week). |
| Deleted tasks and documents | Deleted tasks and documents (with their subtasks and sub-pages) go to the workspace's trash for 30 days, where they can be restored; after 30 days they are permanently deleted automatically. A member can also delete a trashed item permanently at any time. |
| Agent hook events (session records) | By default, for as long as the workspace exists, stored as recorded, including code. A workspace owner can instead choose either (a) a retention period of 30, 90, 180 or 365 days, after which the content of each event is removed, except that events of tasks that are not yet done are kept until the task is done and events cited as evidence in a boundary-crossing record are kept; or (b) discard when used up: file contents, command output and other tool input and output are not stored at all (only their size and a SHA-256 fingerprint), commands are stored shortened to 500 characters without heredoc bodies, prompts and agent messages are stored with any code removed, and the rest of the content is deleted 7 days after it is used up, meaning its task is done (held up to 30 days after completion while we finish reviewing the task) or 30 days pass with no agent activity; content cited as boundary-crossing evidence is held until every such crossing is dispositioned, plus 7 days. When content is removed, the event and its statistics (cost, active time, files touched) are kept. A stricter setting applies only to records stored after the change. |
| Security scan evidence | Repositories cloned for a scan exist only in a short-lived sandbox that is discarded after the scan. The code a scan quoted (flagged lines, call chains, AI-written finding text and the pull-request summary) is cleared 7 days after the pull request is merged or closed, by default; a workspace owner can choose 0, 7, 14 or 30 days. On a pull request that stays open, a scan's code is cleared 30 days after the scan finished. Findings keep their rule, file, line, severity, fingerprint and dispositions. |
| Boundary-crossing records | The original command, diff line and explanations are replaced by a description without code 7 days after the crossing is dispositioned, by default; a workspace owner can choose 7, 30 or 90 days. The category, severity and disposition are kept. A shorter period applies only to crossings dispositioned after the change. |
| Failed pull-request comment syncs | The GitHub webhook payload (comment text and diff context) kept for troubleshooting is deleted 7 days after the sync failed, by default; a workspace owner can choose 7, 30 or 90 days. The event, pull request and error are kept. |
| AI-derived content | Team memory, session summaries and run summaries are generated by AI from agent sessions by default; code blocks and lines of code are removed and credentials are redacted before they are stored. They are kept for as long as the workspace exists unless a workspace owner deletes them; the retention periods above do not apply to them. A workspace owner can turn their generation off in the data retention settings: from then on no new memories, session summaries, run summaries or AI-written task claims are generated (an agent's own report on its work is still kept, and each turn of a session record still gets a one-line summary, stored without code). A workspace owner can also delete what was already generated, after re-typing the workspace name: all team memory (including memories added by hand) with its search embeddings, all session and run summaries, the AI-written task claims and the explanations of claim faithfulness checks. Agent hook events, tasks and documents are not affected. |
| Data stored before these retention rules | Scan evidence, boundary-crossing originals and failed-sync payloads stored before these rules were introduced are not yet removed automatically; contact us if you want them deleted. |
| Integration tokens | Until the integration is disconnected or the workspace is deleted. |
| Technical data and logs | [Log retention period] |
| Backups | Deleted data can persist in encrypted database backups for up to [backup retention period] before it is overwritten. |

When a workspace owner deletes a workspace, the deletion is scheduled 48 hours ahead and every workspace owner is emailed; any owner can cancel it during that window. When the 48 hours pass, we permanently delete the workspace's database records and stored files and keep a deletion receipt recording what was deleted and when (not the deleted content). An owner who needs the data erased immediately, for example to meet an erasure request, can run the scheduled deletion at once from workspace settings. If you uninstall the GitHub App, or remove a repository from it, but keep the workspace, we immediately clear the code stored with the affected security scans (quoted code, call chains, AI-written finding text, pull-request summaries and the scan's audit records), whatever the retention setting; each finding's rule, file, line, severity and dispositions are kept. Other data already collected from GitHub, such as pull-request records, stays with the workspace; contact us if you want it deleted.

## How we protect data

- All traffic to the Service is encrypted in transit with TLS.
- Our database and file storage providers encrypt data at rest.
- Integration access tokens are additionally encrypted with AES-256-GCM before they are stored.
- Sign-in is handled by Clerk; access to workspace data is limited to members of that workspace.
- Security scans of your code run in isolated, short-lived sandboxes.

## Your rights

Depending on where you live, you have the right to:

- **Access** the personal data we hold about you and get a copy of it.
- **Export** your data in a portable, machine-readable format.
- **Correct** inaccurate data. You can edit your profile yourself.
- **Delete** your data. Workspace owners can delete a workspace, and everything in it, from workspace settings (after a 48-hour cancellation window, or at once if they choose). Deleted tasks and documents can be permanently deleted right away from the trash instead of waiting 30 days.
- **Restrict or object** to processing based on our legitimate interests.
- **Withdraw consent** where we rely on it.
- **Complain** to your data protection authority.

To exercise these rights, email [privacy@uselumo.ai]. We answer within 30 days. Workspace owners can export a workspace themselves from workspace settings; that export does not yet include every record we store (our Security page lists what it leaves out). Data the export leaves out, data export for an individual account and deletion of an individual account are currently handled on request by our team; we will verify your identity before acting. For data in a workspace you do not own, we will pass your request to the workspace owner.

California residents: we do not sell or share personal information for cross-context behavioural advertising, and we will not discriminate against you for exercising your privacy rights.
